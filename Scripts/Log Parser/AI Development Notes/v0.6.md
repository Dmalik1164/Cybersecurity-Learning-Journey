AI was used throughout v0.6 as a development aid, mentor and troubleshooting resource rather than as a replacement for my own implementation.

I wrote and implemented the changes myself, using AI primarily to explain concepts when I became stuck, suggest approaches, and help identify errors in my code. I worked through each change interactively and tested the implementation locally before moving onto the next stage.

During the refactoring process, AI helped me understand how to separate responsibilities between functions, how data could be passed between process_log() and analyse_risk(), and how Python variable scope and indentation affected the program.

For the new export functionality, I used AI to understand the Python csv and json modules and the purpose of functions such as csv.writer(), writerow() and json.dump(). I then implemented the export functions myself and tested the generated CSV and JSON files.

AI was also used to troubleshoot errors encountered during development. For example, I worked through scope and indentation errors when separating the risk analysis function, as well as a Windows PermissionError caused by the CSV file being open in Excel.

The development process remained iterative: I wrote code, tested it, encountered errors, investigated the cause with AI assistance, made the changes myself and tested the result again.

AI therefore acted primarily as a learning and debugging tool while the design decisions, coding, testing and final implementation were carried out by me.
